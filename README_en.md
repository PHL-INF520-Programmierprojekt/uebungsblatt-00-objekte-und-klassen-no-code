# Exercise Sheet: Objects and Classes
[Link to German Version](./README.md)

In this exercise sheet, you will learn the difference between objects and classes, and get a basic conceptual understanding of them.

## Exercise: The Great Cookie Caper

In this exercise, you will practice identifying objects, attributes, methods, classes, and relationships in a scenario involving a skilled baker and her customer.

### Scenario

Meet Sarah, the skilled baker who runs her own bakery. Sarah takes pride in her work and enjoys creating fresh baked goods for her customers. One of her regular customers, Alex, often stops by to purchase a dozen chocolate chip cookies. Alex loves the way Sarah's cookies taste, and Sarah has taken note of this preference and has perfected her recipe to suit Alex's taste.

Today, Alex walks into the bakery and places an order for a dozen chocolate chip cookies. Sarah gets to work, carefully measuring out the ingredients and mixing them together to form a perfect batter. She scoops out the dough, places it on a baking sheet, and pops it into the oven. Soon, the delicious aroma of freshly baked cookies fills the bakery.

As soon as the cookies are ready, Sarah pulls them out of the oven and lets them cool. She packages them up in a paper bag and hands them to Alex with a smile. Alex takes a bite of a cookie and savors the sweet, chocolatey goodness. Sarah and Alex are both happy with the outcome of this interaction - Alex gets to enjoy his favorite treat, and Sarah feels proud of the delicious cookies she has created. 

### Tasks

1. Identify at least three objects in this scenario.
2. For each object, identify at least two attributes and one method.
3. Identify the classes to which each object belongs.
4. Describe the relationship between the objects in this scenario.
5. Submit your answers in a markdown file `cookie_caper.md` in the root directory.

## Exercise: The Farmyard Frenzy

### Scenario

One of the cows, Bessie, is particularly fond of the lush grass in the meadow. She spends her days grazing, occasionally taking a break to rest in the shade of a nearby tree. Bob has taken note of Bessie's love of the meadow and has made sure that she always has access to it.

One day, a group of chickens wanders into the meadow, pecking at the grass and generally causing a ruckus. Bessie is annoyed by their presence and lets out a loud "moo" to show her displeasure. The chickens ignore her and continue to scratch in the dirt.

Bob hears the commotion and walks over to investigate. He shoos the chickens away and gives Bessie a pat on the head, telling her that everything is okay. Bessie settles back down to graze, content in the knowledge that she has a caring farmer to look after her.
